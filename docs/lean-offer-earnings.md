# Lean builder offer: earnings scale

Service fees exclude IVA; contribution excludes fixed overhead, financing, and income tax. ## Comparison assumptions

The founder wants the approximate scale of earnings for one small home versus twenty matching apartments. This comparison assumes the founder sells design, configuration, functional testing, and defined handover, while the builder pays suppliers and qualified physical installers directly. The founder has not selected that commercial model. Drawings/specifications alone are a narrower service and would need a separate price and workload estimate.

For comparability, imagine 5–8 lighting points, one motorized curtain, one compatible Alexa screen, necessary hub/connectivity, and a small agreed set of demonstrable controls. One curtain per home is an analytical simplification, not a founder-approved count. The earlier presence/motion sensors and ten-scene package are not automatically included. No exact platform, model, routine count, or local/cloud behavior is selected here.

All hardware, curtain motor/rail/fabric, physical installation, electrical preparation, internet service, and optional extensions are outside these service-fee numbers. The earlier 4,600–5,200 hardware subtotal cannot price this changed package. A complete builder budget remains unknown until the actual scope and supplier/installer quotes exist.

These scenarios use deliberately chosen price and workload assumptions to make the arithmetic understandable. They are not observed Puebla rates, confidence intervals, probabilities, guaranteed minimums, or evidence the builder will accept the fees.

Value founder labor at an illustrative 300/hour. Include all attributable prospecting/quoting, design, coordination, travel time, procurement guidance, configuration, testing, administration, handover, and expected follow-up in the hours. Other variable costs include travel outlays, consumables, transaction costs where applicable, and an incident allowance. These allowances have not been measured; count each cost once. There is no hardware resale income or cost in this model.

Summary of the first six illustrations: one home generates 4,000–7,000 in service revenue and 1,000–3,000 contribution after valuing founder time; twenty generate 33,000–66,000 revenue and 5,000–28,000 contribution. Greater unpriced work can erase those contributions or produce a loss, as the adverse case begins to show. Range endpoints belong to their corresponding rows; do not independently combine favorable assumptions and present the result as the same estimate.

The remainder before owner labor can fund compensation for work plus business return, but it still must cover overhead, tax, financing, and unfinished obligations. It is not automatically distributable cash. If every central-case invoice is collected and the assumed other costs are paid, 50,000 minus 6,000 leaves 44,000 before those remaining obligations: 24,000 represents the assumed value of 80 hours and 20,000 remains as business contribution. These are per-project figures, not monthly income. Elapsed construction time and collection timing are unknown.

The eighty-hour illustration allocates eight common design hours, sixty hours across twenty units for configuration/testing, and twelve hours across sales, coordination, administration, travel, handover, and follow-up. That last allowance is tight and depends on batched access, ready installations, reusable documents, and limited later visits. Twenty separate resident appointments may invalidate it. Measuring the whole workflow is essential before offering a volume fee. Each additional twenty hours costs another 6,000 at the assumed hourly value.

## How to price repetition

A common design fee plus a fee per commissioned home makes the builder's volume benefit visible without treating twenty apartments as one installation. An illustrative twenty-home structure is 3,000–6,000 for shared design plus 1,500–3,000 per unit, totaling 33,000–66,000. The central example is 5,000 plus twenty times 2,250, totaling 50,000. These are price hypotheses for a batch, not a universal schedule or an additional charge on top of the table. Standalone-home scenarios are separate all-in service-price hypotheses.

Discount only work actually saved through identical layouts, tested equipment, grouped visits, and a repeatable delivery process. Keep each home's account ownership, verification, and resident handover separate. Construction changes, new layouts, repeat visits caused by unreadiness, and later customization need defined treatment in the eventual scope. Do not make payment for completed work depend on the builder selling all homes.

## Offer and validation implications

The founder's proposed lighting/curtain/screen demonstration is a candidate way to show an immediate convenience. Compare it with a simpler lighting/curtain offer using an existing phone or compatible speaker to establish whether the screen earns its extra cost. Screen inclusion must be clear in any eventual demonstration and delivery description. Do not show optional equipment as included in every sold apartment.

Local smart-home marketing already exists: ValorCity's HEM page advertises an Alexa system and digital lock in Lomas. This supports the existence of the marketing language, not sales uplift, a validated price, or a novel competitive advantage. Our candidate advantage is a defined working outcome delivered with predictable cost and support; it remains to be validated.

Recommend exploring one paid representative installation, then measured repetition in the same development, before expanding to another builder. The first commercial test is acceptance of the complete equipment/installation/service budget for specified functions, followed by observed delivery hours and follow-up cost. Agree a spending/hour cap and acceptance conditions before execution. No pilot or spending is authorized by this document.

For the lean baseline, prioritize clear manual operation, a few predictable functions, and a defined support boundary. Irrigation and water-leak alerts can be separately scoped where useful; neither is automatically maintenance-free. A water sensor detects water and may trigger notifications or linked devices; it does not itself provide a complete shutoff system.
