Assumption pending clarification: the requested percentage means service fees divided by equipment acquisition cost, on the same tax basis. It does not mean percentage of property sale price, hardware markup, or profit margin.

A percentage-only fee falls when equipment gets cheaper even if design, travel, commissioning, testing, and handover take the same time. Use a scope-based fee with a labor/cost floor; the percentage is a comparison measure, not the pricing engine.

Provisional prices to test with this exact scope and the stated cost assumptions:

- **First apartment / prototype: 5,000 in service fees**, covering the initial adaptation of the design and scenes, configuration, testing, and handover for that apartment. Approximately 96–109% of the listed hardware cost.
- **Subsequent matching apartment: 3,500 in service fees**, only if the prototype, models, functions, network requirements, and delivery workflow are substantially reusable and the measured workload supports this fee. Approximately 67–76% of the listed hardware cost.

These are proposed trial prices, not evidence of willingness to pay or a universal minimum. Unknown workload or service obligations may require higher fees. A smaller device count alone does not establish a proportional reduction in hours. New layouts, new platform combinations, or additional functions require a revised scope and estimate.

The baseline commercial model remains builder-direct equipment purchase and paid services. If equipment resale is later selected, retain a separately costed service component and calculate the additional supply charge from actual procurement, logistics, financing, and warranty exposure. Do not treat the approximately 70% service-to-hardware ratio as a hardware resale markup or a profit margin.

## Illustrative owner-time economics

The founder has not provided an hourly target or measured delivery hours. The following are invented planning assumptions, not Puebla labor rates: value all founder time at 300 per hour, and allow 500 per apartment for nonlabor variable costs such as travel, consumables, and expected incident expenses. The allowance is not a measured warranty reserve. Hours include preparation, coordination, trips, setup, scene tuning, testing, administration, handover, and a provisional allocation of later support; do not count any item twice.

| Scenario | Service fee | Total founder hours | Imputed labor cost | Other variable costs | Contribution before fixed costs, financing, and income tax |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| First prototype | 5,000 | 12 | 3,600 | 500 | 900 |
| Repeat, lower workload | 3,500 | 4 | 1,200 | 500 | 1,800 |
| Repeat, base illustration | 3,500 | 6 | 1,800 | 500 | 1,200 |
| Repeat, adverse workload | 3,500 | 10 | 3,000 | 500 | 0 |

At 3,500, with the assumed 300 per hour and 500 other cost, ten hours consume the full fee before fixed costs. This is a break-even warning under assumptions, not an acceptable workload target. At six hours the service contribution margin is approximately 34.3%, not 70%. A change to the founder's hourly value or actual incident costs changes every conclusion.

For an actual target contribution margin:

`service_fee = (all_founder_hours * internal_hourly_cost + other_variable_costs) / (1 - target_contribution_margin)`

The target must cover the fixed cost burden and desired business return at realistic volume. None has been selected by the founder. Use the formula only with a target below 1 and correctly categorized costs. Model imputed labor separately from actual cash payroll and owner withdrawals.

## Starting and growing

1. **Scope and measure one paid prototype.** Confirm exact models, conventional versus smart bulbs, electrical readiness, connectivity, approximately ten named scenes and their expected behavior, handover audience, and included assistance. Obtain the electrical delta and agree the complete scope and payment terms before implementation. Test the actual delivered functions and recoverability, with permission for any deliberate service interruption. Record all hours and extra visits. Acceptance requires working agreed functions, a user who can operate them, known limitations, and enough actual fee to pay the costs.
2. **Repeat within the same development.** Reuse the tested specification and documentation, while independently setting up each dwelling's accounts and checking its site conditions. Train the builder's responsible installer on the agreed preparation requirements. Apply the repeat fee only when the measured reduction in work exists. Reprice or narrow the scope if added hours consume the contribution; never assume volume alone will cure a loss-making unit.
3. **Expand delivery capacity after repeatability is demonstrated.** Calculate capacity from available hours after sales, administration, and support divided by observed hours per apartment. Arrange paid qualified installation/configuration assistance for a demonstrated bottleneck, with training and quality checks, while the founder retains their chosen design/configuration role. Record builder acquisition effort, delivery hours, incidents, collections, and contribution for each project before adding further developments.
4. **Add extensions and supply margin selectively.** Evaluate curtains, additional zones, supported ecosystem setup, and defined later services as separately scoped offers. Consider supplying equipment only when additional contribution, advance-payment terms, supplier remedies, and cash exposure justify it. Purchases for speculative inventory or direct import are not part of this starting proposal.

This growth path is advisory. No pilot, staffing, supplier contract, customer communication, equipment purchase, or live-home work has been authorized. No quantitative loss limit or business launch target has been agreed.
